FI `a²+b⁴`; Heath-Brown `x³+2y³`). Its sole distinctive content was the observation that **TPC follows

## Part 2 — Atomic lemmas

### PROVEN TRUE

**`L1 [SPARSE]`** — *TPC follows from the existence of infinitely many twins in any infinite subset of ℕ.*
**Test:** logic. Trivially true. This is G3's only distinctive content. **Not a gate.**

**`L2 [DENSITY]`** — *A degree-`d` form on `a,b ≤ B` yields `≍ B²/(log B)²` expected twins → ∞.*
**Test:** heuristic count; values are of size `B^d`, so both-prime probability is `≍ (log B)^{-2}`.
Sparsity costs nothing. **Not a gate.**

**`L5 [DIM-1]`** — *The shifted-prime sequence `a_n = Λ(n−2)` has sieve dimension exactly 1.*
**Test:** direct. `Σ_{n≤x, d|n} Λ(n−2) = Σ_{p ≡ −2 (d)} log p ~ x/φ(d)`, so `g(d) = 1/φ(d)` for odd `d`
and `g(2) = 0`; hence `g(p) = 1/(p−1) ~ 1/p`.
> **REFUTES MY OWN PHASE-1 PREDICTION.** I predicted sieve dimension 2 would bite, on the grounds that
> `n(n+2)` has two linear factors. It does not: in the *shifted-prime* formulation the dimension is 1 —
> exactly what Bombieri and FI require. Ford's notes confirm the criterion (*"the product for H converges
> to a nonzero constant iff g has sieve dimension 1"*).

**`L6 [LEVEL-OK]`** — *The Type-I hypothesis for `a_n = Λ(n−2)` does not collapse; it is exactly
Elliott–Halberstam.*
**Test:** direct. `Σ_{n ≡ 0 (d)} Λ(n−2) = Σ_{p ≡ −2 (d)} log p`, i.e. primes in APs.
> **REFUTES MY OWN PHASE-0 PREDICTION.** I predicted the level-of-distribution hypothesis would collapse
> because the sequence is prime-supported. **That was an error**: I conflated *prime-supported* (where
> `d | p` forces `d = p`, one term) with ***shifted*-prime-supported** (where `d | p+2` is a nontrivial
> congruence, perfectly well distributed). **The shift saves it.** G3 was substantially more alive than
> my plan allowed.

**`L7 [CONST]`** — *FI's constant for this sequence is exactly the Hardy–Littlewood constant.*
`H = Π_p (1−g(p))(1−1/p)^{-1}`; for odd `p`, `(1 − 1/(p−1))·p/(p−1) = p(p−2)/(p−1)² = 1 − 1/(p−1)²`, and
the `p = 2` factor is `(1−0)(1/2)^{-1} = 2`. So `H = 2·Π_{p>2}(1 − 1/(p−1)²) = 2Π₂`.
**Test:** algebra above, plus numerics — `H = 1.3203236744` vs `2Π₂ = 1.3203236744`, rel. diff `2.19e-15`
(both at cutoff `2·10⁶`; the *algebraic* identity is the certification, per the A23 lesson about
self-comparison).
**Consequence:** the architecture is fully coherent — FI's conclusion `Σ a_n Λ(n) ~ HX` would be
**literally the twin prime asymptotic, with the correct constant.** G3 is not killed by any structural
mismatch.

**`L9 [PARAM ≡ ITER]`** — *The "imposed-parameter" architecture and the "iterate FI" architecture are the
same object.* Fouvry–Iwaniec's `x²+p²` has one primality **imposed** (the parameter `p`, summed over a
known set) and one **detected** (the value). Imposing one condition for twins means summing over primes
`p` and detecting `p+2` — which is precisely `a_n = Λ(n−2)`. **Test:** structural. The two architectures
stand or fall together.

**`L10 [COMMENSURATE-INVARIANT]`** — ***the decisive lemma.*** *For any family `F`, the pair `(F, F+2)`
has constant difference, and restriction to a subfamily cannot change this.*
**Test:** compare against every **proven** `k=2, m=2` instance of the prime-tuples conjecture:

| result | `f₁` | `f₂` | `f₂ − f₁` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fouvry–Iwaniec `x²+p²` | `x` | `x²+Ny²` | non-constant |
| Heath-Brown–Li `a²+p⁴` | `y` | `x²+y⁴` | non-constant |
| He, `p²+Ny²` | `x` | `x²+Ny²` | non-constant |
| **G3 target** | `F(a,b)` | `F(a,b)+2` | **2 — CONSTANT** |

In every proven case the two polynomials are non-commensurate and one is linear in a subset of the
variables, so its primality can be *imposed*, freeing the other variable to supply the bilinear average.
For `(F, F+2)` neither can be imposed (they are the same object up to a constant) and the difference is
constant — which is exactly the infinite-Gowers-complexity condition already recorded at `A11(b)`/`A14`.
**`f₂ − f₁ = 2` is invariant under every restriction of the family.**

> **This closes G3 at its root.** `[SPARSE]` — G3's *only* distinctive content — buys parameters but is
> **provably orthogonal to the property that makes TPC hard.** The gate does not fail on a technicality;
> its distinguishing move cannot reach the obstruction.

### PROVEN FALSE

**`L3 [JOINT-QUAD]`** — *There is no nondegenerate binary quadratic form `F` with `F+2` also a norm form
under an affine substitution.* **FALSE (no such `F`).**
**Test (a), exhaustive:** all `(α,β,γ,δ,ε,ζ)` with `|coef| ≤ 6` and `E ∈ 1..6` in
`F + 2 = (αa+βb+γ)² + E(δa+εb+ζ)²`, requiring both linear coefficients to vanish, constant `= 2`, and
nondegenerate quadratic part → **0 hits**.
**Test (b), proof:** if `F + 2 = G∘L` with `F, G` nondegenerate and `L` affine, matching quadratic parts
forces `L` invertible; the linear part of `G∘L` at `0` is `∇G(L(0))·L₀`, which must vanish, so `∇G(L(0)) = 0`,
so `L(0)` is `G`'s unique critical point, where `G = 0`. Then the constant term is `0`, not `2`. ∎

**`L4 [JOINT-ESCAPE]`** — *The degenerate escape (inner map avoiding the origin, e.g. `d ≡ const`) does not
exist.* **FALSE.** **Test:** it requires `a² + Db² + k` to be a perfect square *as a polynomial*, which
forces `D = 0`, i.e. `F` is not a binary form. Search over `E ∈ 1..6`, `D ∈ 1..19`, `z ∈ −4..4` → no
solutions.

### SCOPED / OPEN-CAPPED

**`L8 [TYPEII-CIRC]`** — *FI's bilinear hypothesis, applied to `a_n = Λ(n−2)`, is a binary prime
correlation of the same species as `C5`.*
**Basis:** the sourced plain-language characterisation — *"Roughly speaking the bilinear forms condition
asks that `aₙ` never looks like `μ(n)` (or its cousin `λ(n)`) on any arithmetic progression"* (X. He,
*Primes of the form p²+Ny²*, Harvard senior thesis, §2.4; text extracted and read directly). For
`a_n = Λ(n−2)` this demands `Σ_{n ≡ a (q)} μ(n)Λ(n−2) = o(·)` uniformly in APs — the **μ–Λ shifted
correlation, uniform in progressions**, which is the `μ`–`Λ` analogue of `C5` and is open.
**STATUS: NOT CERTIFIED.** This rests on a plain-language gloss, not FI's formal inequality, which I
could not obtain. Recorded as **OPEN-capped**; `L10` does not depend on it.

Same source, worth recording: *"to the author's knowledge the exact formulation of the Asymptotic Sieve

**G3 is CLOSED.** Not by sieve dimension (`L5` refutes that), not by level of distribution (`L6` refutes
that), and not by the absence of a family in the literature — but by `L10`: the shift by 2 is a constant,

- **Not progress. Not a bound. `C5` untouched.**

**Deliverable — the `C3` upgrade.** `C3` currently reads *"none known for n(n+2)"*, flagged as an
absence-of-citation fact. A24 supplies a structural replacement:

> The FI framework **does** apply to the twin problem in the shifted-prime formulation — correct sieve
> dimension (1), correct constant (`2Π₂`), non-degenerate Type-I hypothesis (= Elliott–Halberstam).
> What fails is not the framework's applicability but its reach: every proven instance requires the two
> polynomials to be non-commensurate so that one primality condition can be imposed, and `f₂ − f₁ = 2`
> is constant and restriction-invariant. **Thinning the family cannot help, for any family.**

That is a genuine strengthening of a node, and it is negative.
