## Part 2 — The bound at the crux quantity (the positive finding)

### 2.1 Proposition A22.1 (shift 2, \(d=1\), all scales, unconditional)

> Let \(c>0\) be the minimum of the eight lower densities furnished by
> Matomäki–Radziwiłł–Tao (Forum Math. Sigma **4** (2016) e14) for the sign patterns of
> \((\lambda(n),\lambda(n+1),\lambda(n+2))\). Then
> \[
>   \limsup_{x\to\infty}\ \frac1x\Big|\sum_{n\le x}\lambda(n)\lambda(n+2)\Big|\ \le\ 1-8c\ <\ 1 .
> \]

**Proof.** The eight sets \(N_{\epsilon_1\epsilon_2\epsilon_3}=\{n:(\lambda(n),\lambda(n+1),\lambda(n+2))=(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3)\}\)
partition \(\mathbb N\). Put
\(P(x)=\#\{n\le x:\lambda(n)\lambda(n+2)=+1\}\) and \(M(x)=\#\{n\le x:\lambda(n)\lambda(n+2)=-1\}\);
then \(P(x)+M(x)=x+O(1)\) and \(\sum_{n\le x}\lambda(n)\lambda(n+2)=P(x)-M(x)\).
\(P\) is the union of the four patterns with \(\epsilon_1\epsilon_3=+1\) and \(M\) of the
four with \(\epsilon_1\epsilon_3=-1\); since liminf is superadditive on disjoint unions,
\(\liminf P(x)/x\ge 4c\) and \(\liminf M(x)/x\ge 4c\). Hence
\(\limsup (P-M)/x\le \limsup (x-2M)/x\le 1-8c\), and symmetrically
\(\liminf(P-M)/x\ge-(1-8c)\). \(\square\)

Note \(8c\le1\) automatically, so the statement is never vacuous and never false.

### 2.2 Proposition A22.2 (exact dilation identity; the modulus-2 class)

> For every \(x\), **exactly**:
> \[
>   \sum_{n\le x,\ 2\mid n}\lambda(n)\lambda(n+2)\;=\;\sum_{m\le \lfloor x/2\rfloor}\lambda(m)\lambda(m+1).
> \]
> Consequently, with \(\delta_1>0\) the absolute constant of the shift-1 bound quoted
> from Tao/[MR],
> \(\big|\sum_{n\le x,\,2\mid n}\lambda(n)\lambda(n+2)\big|\le(1-\delta_1)\,\frac x2\)
> for large \(x\) — a nontrivial bound at \((a,d)=(0,2)\).

**Proof.** Put \(n=2m\). Complete multiplicativity and \(\lambda(2)=-1\) give
\(\lambda(2m)=-\lambda(m)\) and \(\lambda(2m+2)=\lambda(2)\lambda(m+1)=-\lambda(m+1)\);
the two signs cancel. The map \(m\mapsto2m\) is a bijection from \(m\le\lfloor x/2\rfloor\)
onto the even \(n\le x\). \(\square\)

### 2.3 The exact reach of the dilation identity on the AP axis (sharp, rigorous)

> The identity of A22.2 generalises to
> \(\sum_{n\le x,\ d\mid n}\lambda(n)\lambda(n+h)=\lambda(d)^2\sum_{m\le x/d}\lambda(m)\lambda(m+h/d)
>  =\sum_{m\le x/d}\lambda(m)\lambda(m+h/d)\)
> **iff \(d\mid h\)** — because \(dm+h\) is of the form \(d\cdot(\text{integer})\) only then.
> For \(h=2\) this means **\(d\in\{1,2\}\)**, and nothing else.

So the multiplicative-dilation route reaches exactly two of the pairs \((a,d)\) over
which C5 quantifies: \(d=1\), and \((a,d)=(0,2)\). The odd class mod 2 is **not**
reached: from \(|\Sigma_{\text{all}}|\le(1-8c)x\) and \(|\Sigma_{\text{even}}|\le(1-\delta_1)x/2\)
one gets only \(|\Sigma_{\text{odd}}|\le(1-8c)x+(1-\delta_1)x/2>x/2\), which is worse
than trivial. **Recorded honestly: no nontrivial bound for the odd class.**

### 2.4 What this is and is not

* It **is** unconditional, Cesàro (not logarithmically averaged), valid at **all** large
  \(x\), and about the **exact** quantity C5 names.
* It is **not** new mathematics — it is a four-line corollary of a 2016 theorem, and
  Tao states the shift-1 version explicitly in print. Its novelty is **only relative to
  this project's claim graph**, where it repairs a false premise.
* It is **not** progress toward TPC. C5 needs \(\delta(x)\to0\); this gives a fixed
  \(1-\delta\). The gap between "beat the trivial bound by a constant" and "\(o(1)\)"
  is the entire parity problem.
* \(\delta\) is **not made explicit here**. MRT's densities are not extracted in this
  step; whether their method gives an effective constant is left OPEN and flagged for
  DISTILL. The numerics below show the *truth* is near \(\delta=1\), which says nothing
  about the *theorem's* constant.

## Part 3 — The correct spectral framing (correcting A21 handle III)

A21's [TRACE-EQUIV] (OPEN) asks for a structural trace-class \(A_x\) with
\(\mathrm{trace}(A_x)=C5(x)\) and \(\|A_x\|_1\) small, and A21 found only: rank-one
(forbidden: resums \(\sum c\)), diagonal (\(\|\cdot\|_1=x\), no saving), Hankel/Toeplitz
(norm grows, trace wrong). **The literature reaches a bound with a different spectral
invariant.** Verbatim from Pilatte arXiv:2310.19357v2, §1.2 (extracted text, PDF glyph
artifacts cleaned):

> "Using the multiplicativity of \(\lambda\), Tao [22] showed that the problem of
> bounding \(\sum_{n\le x}\frac1n\lambda(n)\lambda(n+1)\) reduces to bounding
> \[\mathbb E_{p\in\mathcal P}\sum_{n\le x}\lambda(n)\lambda(n+p)\Big(1_{p\mid n}-\frac1p\Big),\]
> where \(\mathcal P\subset[1,\exp(\sqrt{\log x})]\) is a set of primes. Helfgott and
> Radziwiłł [11] interpreted the above expression as the matrix product
> \(\lambda^{\mathsf T}A\lambda\) where \(\lambda:=(\lambda(1),\dots,\lambda(x))^{\mathsf T}\)
> and \(A\) is the matrix with entries
> \[A_{mn}:=\begin{cases}1_{p\mid n}-\frac1p&\text{if }|m-n|=p\in\mathcal P,\\ 0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\]
> Hence, it is sufficient to bound the eigenvalues of the matrix \(A\), or the
> eigenvalues of its restriction \(A|_X\) to some very dense subset \(X\subset\{1,\dots,x\}\).
> Using a high trace method, Helfgott and Radziwiłł [11] managed to obtain the bound
> \((\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}1/p)^{1/2+o(1)}\) for the largest eigenvalue of such a matrix,
> which is essentially the best possible."

and Pilatte's own improvement replaces single primes by products \(d=p_1\cdots p_k\),
\(k\asymp\log\log x\), with \(\widetilde A_{mn}=\prod_{p\mid d}(1_{p\mid n}-1/p)\) when
\(|m-n|=d\in\mathcal D_k\), proving all eigenvalues of \(\widetilde A|_X\) are
\((\sum_{d\in\mathcal D_k}1/d)^{1/2+o(1)}\) with \(\sum_{d\in\mathcal D_k}1/d\gg(\log x)^c\).

**Three structural corrections to the project's framing, all load-bearing:**

* **(F1) The invariant is the operator norm, not the trace norm**, and the pairing is
  the **quadratic form** \(\lambda^{\mathsf T}A\lambda\), not the trace. \(A\) is
  explicit and elementary — no zeta, no \(F_1\) geometry, no Trap A. Its entries are
  \(1_{p\mid n}-1/p\).
* **(F2) The evaluation is genuinely structural.** The eigenvalue bound is proved by a
  high-trace / non-backtracking (Ihara–Bass) argument on the graph, i.e. by counting
  walks — it does **not** resum \(\sum c(n)\). This is exactly the structural clause
  [TRACE-EQUIV] demands, and it is met.
* **(F3) It yields an actual bound** — the current record \((\log x)^{1-c}\).

So A21's handle III is not merely unresolved: **it was pointed at the wrong operator
ideal.** This does **not** license relabelling [TRACE-EQUIV]; that node stays OPEN
exactly as it is (its statement is about the trace norm). What changes is that the
project now records that the *productive* spectral question is the operator-norm one,
and that it is already answered in the literature at \(d=1\), log-averaged.

**Where the \(\lambda\)-blindness objection of A18/A19 goes.** A18/A19 concluded that
every natural (gauge-invariant) functional is \(\lambda\)-blind and every
\(\lambda\)-seeing one is C5-by-definition. The HR/Pilatte construction is the third
case A19's [CYC-WALL] said does not exist — but note **it is not a functional at all**:
the matrix \(A\) is \(\lambda\)-blind by construction (Pilatte: *"This key step
effectively suppresses the role of the Liouville function in the problem"*, §3.2), and
\(\lambda\) enters only as an arbitrary \(\pm1\) test vector. The bound is
\(|\lambda^{\mathsf T}A\lambda|\le\|A|_X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\cdot x\), which holds for
*every* \(\pm1\) vector. **That is why it evades the gauge dichotomy and also why it
cannot reach \(o(1)\)**: the method never uses any property of \(\lambda\) beyond
\(|\lambda|\le1\), so its saving is capped by \(\|A|_X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), and \(A\) is a
fixed arithmetic object with \(\sum_{d}1/d\) at most a power of \(\log x\). This is a
clean, non-metaphorical statement of the ceiling, and it agrees with Pilatte's own
sentence: *"It appears that saving a fixed power of the logarithm is the best that is
achievable with current techniques."*

## Part 4 — Reach: how far this mechanism can get toward C5 (the negative finding)

C5 demands \(o(1)\) **uniformly over \(d\le x^{1-\varepsilon}\) and all residues**.
Three elementary, rigorous facts bound the mechanism's reach on the \(d\)-axis.

**(R-i) The matrix has no edges inside a residue class.** Every nonzero entry of \(A\)
(resp. \(\widetilde A\)) has \(|m-n|=p\in\mathcal P\) (resp. \(=d\in\mathcal D_k\)). If
\(m\equiv n\pmod D\) then \(D\mid|m-n|\). With \(\mathcal P\subset(H_0,H]\) a set of
**primes**, \(D\mid p\) forces \(D=1\) or \(D=p\). Hence for every modulus \(D\) that is
not \(1\) and not a member of \(\mathcal P\), **\(A\) restricted to any residue class
mod \(D\) is the zero matrix**, and for \(D=p\in\mathcal P\) the restricted graph has
edges from a single prime only (degree \(\le2\): no expansion). Audited in Part 5(5a).

**(R-ii) Class-preserving dilations need primes \(\equiv1\pmod D\), which do not exist
below \(D\).** Tao's reduction moves \(n\) to \(pn\); on residues this maps the class
\(a\) to the class \(pa\pmod D\). The class is preserved iff \(p\equiv1\pmod D\). Every
prime \(p\equiv1\pmod D\) satisfies \(p=1+kD\ge1+D>D\) (\(k\ge1\), since \(p=1\) is not
prime). So if the mechanism's primes are confined to \(p\le H\) and \(D\ge H\), there
are **none**. Quantitatively the mechanism's gain is governed by
\(V_D:=\sum_{p\le H,\ p\equiv1(D)}1/p\) and it is useful only when \(V_D>1\). By
Brun–Titchmarsh and partial summation \(V_D\ll(\log\log H)/\varphi(D)\), so
\(V_D>1\) forces \(\varphi(D)\ll\log\log H\), i.e. **\(D=O(\log\log H)\)**. With
\(H\le\exp(\sqrt{\log x})\) (Tao's range, as quoted by Pilatte) this is
\(D=O(\log\log x)\). Audited in Part 5(5b).

**(R-iii) The character-twisted route trades \(D\) against a power of \(\log\).** One
can avoid (R-ii) by writing \(1_{n\equiv a(D)}=\varphi(D)^{-1}\sum_\chi\bar\chi(a)\chi(n)\)
and applying the machinery to the completely multiplicative \(g=\lambda\chi\) (which is
non-pretentious for every \(\chi\), since
\(\mathbb D(\lambda\chi,\psi n^{it})^2=\sum_p(1+\mathrm{Re}\,\chi\bar\psi(p)p^{-it})/p\)
diverges). But the twist is bounded against the **full** range: it produces
\(|S_{a,D}(x)|\ll x\cdot\text{(saving)}\), whereas C5 needs \(o(x/D)\). Hence one needs
\(\text{saving}\ll1/D\). With the best saving available — a fixed power of \(\log x\),
which Pilatte states is the ceiling of current techniques — this gives
**\(D\ll(\log x)^{c}\)**. And even that is conditional: uniformity of the eigenvalue
bound in \(\chi\) with \(D\) growing is not proven anywhere I can verify, so the
*unconditional* reach of the twisted route is \(D=O(1)\).

**Conclusion (the measurement).** The best proven parity-breaking mechanism reaches
* \(d=1\): log-averaged \((\log x)^{1-c}\) [Pilatte]; unweighted at almost all scales
  \(\ll x/(\log X)^{c/2}\) outside a set of logarithmic density \(O((\log X)^{-c/2})\)
  [Pilatte, Remark 2.8, extracted verbatim];
* \(d\) fixed: log-averaged \(o(\log x)\) [Tao Thm 1.2 covers \(\lambda(a_1n+b_1)\lambda(a_2n+b_2)\), and the twisted case by Elliott form];
* \(d\) growing: **polylogarithmic at best, conditionally; \(O(1)\) unconditionally.**

C5 needs \(d\) up to \(x^{1-\varepsilon}\). **The deficit is a full power of \(x\)** —
not an \(\varepsilon\), not a constant, not a \(\log\). That is the sharpest statement
of the \(d\)-uniformity wall the project has produced, and unlike the previous wall
statements (A18/A19/A20/A21) it is a statement about a mechanism that **actually works**,
so it measures a real distance rather than recording a collapse.

## Part 5 — Audits (code + captured output)

All five audits below were produced by one script, run once. Liouville computed to
\(N=10^7\) by a prime-power sieve; the script self-checks
\(\lambda(2)=-1,\ \lambda(4)=+1\) and complete multiplicativity on samples before use.

### Code

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
# A22 audits: import of the sign-pattern route and the HR/Pilatte expansion route onto C5.
import numpy as np

N = 10**7

def liouville(N):
    """lam[n] = (-1)^Omega(n) for n=1..N, by prime-power sieve."""
    Om = np.zeros(N+1, dtype=np.int8)
    is_c = np.zeros(N+1, dtype=bool)
    for p in range(2, N+1):
        if is_c[p]:
            continue
        is_c[p*p::p] = True
        pk = p
        while pk <= N:
            Om[pk::pk] += 1
            if pk > N//p:
                break
            pk *= p
    lam = np.where(Om % 2 == 0, 1, -1).astype(np.int8)
    lam[0] = 0
    return lam

lam = liouville(N); lam64 = lam.astype(np.int64)
assert lam[2] == -1 and lam[3] == -1 and lam[4] == 1 and lam[6] == 1 and lam[12] == -1
for (a, b) in [(3,5),(7,11),(10,21),(35,6),(100,7)]:
    assert lam[a*b] == lam[a]*lam[b], (a, b)

# AUDIT 1: exact identity  sum_{n<=x,2|n} lam(n)lam(n+2) = sum_{m<=x/2} lam(m)lam(m+1)
for x in [10, 101, 1000, 12345, 10**5, 999999, 4*10**6, 9999997]:
    ns = np.arange(2, x+1, 2); lhs = int(np.sum(lam64[ns]*lam64[ns+2]))
    ms = np.arange(1, x//2+1); rhs = int(np.sum(lam64[ms]*lam64[ms+1]))
    print(f"  x={x:>8}: LHS={lhs:>8}  RHS={rhs:>8}  equal={lhs==rhs}")

# AUDIT 2: Tao's log-dilation identity, exact:
#   sum_{n<=x,p|n} lam(n)lam(n+p)/n = (1/p) sum_{m<=x/p} lam(m)lam(m+1)/m
x = 3*10**6; inv = 1.0/np.arange(1, N+1, dtype=np.float64)
for p in [2,3,5,7,11,101]:
    ns = np.arange(p, x+1, p)
    L = float(np.sum(lam64[ns]*lam64[ns+p]*inv[ns-1]))

    print(f"  p={p:>4}: log-LHS={L: .10f} log-RHS={R: .10f} |diff|={abs(L-R):.2e}"
          f"   | Cesaro LHS={LC:>9.0f} RHS={RC:>9.0f} ratio={LC/RC: .3f}")

# AUDIT 3: empirical sign-pattern densities and the implied constant
for X in [10**5, 10**6, 10**7-3]:
    a = lam[1:X+1]; b = lam[2:X+2]; c = lam[3:X+3]
    counts = {(s1,s2,s3): int(np.count_nonzero((a==s1)&(b==s2)&(c==s3)))

    tot = sum(counts.values()); cmin = min(counts.values())/tot
    S2 = int(np.sum(a.astype(np.int64)*c.astype(np.int64)))
```
