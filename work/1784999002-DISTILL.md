### 0.1 Tao 2016, verbatim (`chowla.tex` lines 127–132) — the load-bearing citation

> `\begin{theorem}[Logarithmically averaged Chowla conjecture]\label{lach}  Let $a_1,a_2$ be natural`
> `numbers, and let $b_1,b_2$ be integers such that $a_1 b_2 - a_2 b_1 \neq 0$.  Let`
> `$1 \leq \omega(x) \leq x$ be a quantity depending on $x$ that goes to infinity as $x \to \infty$.`
> `Then one has`
> `\[ \sum_{x/\omega(x) < n \leq x} \frac{\lambda(a_1 n + b_1) \lambda(a_2 n+b_2)}{n} = o( \log \omega(x) ) \]`
> `as $n \to \infty$. \end{theorem}`

**The \((a_1n+b_1,\ a_2n+b_2)\) form is what matters: it covers arithmetic progressions.** With

\(\sum_{n\le x,\,n\equiv a\,(L)}\lambda(n)\lambda(n+j)/n=o(\log x)\) for every fixed modulus \(L\),
residue \(a\) and shift \(j\ge1\). **Unconditional.** (Tao's Remark: "Our arguments are in principle
effective … however we did not do so here", `chowla.tex` line 169 — effectivity is claimed but no
number, exactly as with MRT Remark 1.7. Nothing here depends on a number.)

### A2.1 The exact identity (elementary, and the hinge of everything below)

### A2.2 The cap

> **[E4] THE CAP.** Let \(S\) be a multiplicative support of natural density \(\sigma>0\) and let
> \(\chi\) be a real Dirichlet character (possibly trivial), \(f=\lambda\chi\cdot 1_S\). Then
> \[ \delta^\*(f)\ :=\ \liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{{\rm SC}_f(x)}{x}\ \le\ \frac\sigma2 . \]
> In particular **no theorem of MR Corollary 3's shape — "\(f\) has \(\ge\delta x\) sign changes for
> all large \(x\)" — can ever have \(\delta>\sigma/2\), however much MR's proof is improved.**

*Proof.* **Step 1 (periodic case).** Let \(S\) be periodic mod \(M\), \(\chi\) of modulus \(q\),
\(L={\rm lcm}(M,q)\). The successor gap \(j_a:=a_+-a\) and the sign \(\varepsilon_a:=\chi(a)\chi(a+j_a)\in\{\pm1\}\)
are constant on each class \(a\bmod L\) meeting \(S\). Hence
\(A_f(x)=\sum_{a}\varepsilon_a\sum_{n\le x,\,n\equiv a\,(L)}\lambda(n)\lambda(n+j_a)\), a **finite**
\(\pm1\)-combination. By Tao 2016 Thm 1.2 (§0.1) each inner sum, log-weighted, is \(o(\log x)\), so
\(\sum_{n\le x,n\in S}f(n)f(n_+)/n=o(\log x)\).

**Step 2 (Abel).** Suppose \(A_f(t)\le ct\) for all \(t\ge t_0\). Then
\(\sum_{n\le x}a(n)/n=A_f(x)/x+\int_1^x A_f(t)t^{-2}dt\le c+c\log(x/t_0)+O_{t_0}(1)=c\log x+O(1)\).
Comparing with Step 1 forces \(c\ge0\). I.e. \(\limsup_x A_f(x)/x\ \ge\ 0\).

**Step 3 ([E1]).** \(\liminf_x{\rm SC}_f(x)/x=\tfrac12\big(\sigma-\limsup_x A_f(x)/x\big)\le\sigma/2\). ∎

**Step 4 (removing periodicity — the cap holds for *every* multiplicative support).** For any
\(\varepsilon>0\) pick a periodic \(S'\) with \(\overline{\rm dens}(S\triangle S')<\varepsilon\)
(possible by §A6's truncation lemma). Pairs of \(S\) with gap \(>G\) number \(\le x/G\); for gap
\(\le G\), the successor of \(n\) differs between \(S\) and \(S'\) only if \(S\triangle S'\) meets
\((n,n+G]\), which happens for \(\le G\varepsilon x+o(x)\) values of \(n\). Hence
\(|A_f^{S}(t)-A_f^{S'}(t)|\le 2(t/G+G\varepsilon t)+o(t)\); take \(G=\varepsilon^{-1/2}\) to get
\(\le 5\varepsilon^{1/2}t\). Steps 1–2 applied to \(S'\) then give
\(\limsup A_f^{S}(t)/t\ge-5\varepsilon^{1/2}\) for every \(\varepsilon\), hence \(\ge0\). ∎

### 5.2 The repair: for multiplicative supports, \(R\) **does** have a natural density

> **[E7].** Let \(S=\{n: v_p(n)\in E_p\ \forall p\}\) with \(0\in E_p\) and \(\sum_{p:1\notin E_p}1/p<\infty\)
> (MR Cor 3's hypothesis, by MR's own equivalence at source line 1653). Then for every \(h\), the
> gap-\(h\) consecutive-pair set \(R\) has a natural density.

*Proof.* Put \(S_{y,K}:=\{n:\ \forall p\le y,\ \min(v_p(n),K)\in E_p^{(K)}\}\): a **periodic** set
(period \(\prod_{p\le y}p^{K+1}\)). Then
\(\overline{\rm dens}(S\triangle S_{y,K})\le\sum_{p\le y}p^{-K}+\sum_{p>y}\big(1_{1\notin E_p}/p+1/p^{2}\big)\),
which \(\to0\) on letting \(K\to\infty\) then \(y\to\infty\) (the middle sum converges by hypothesis,
the last always). Membership in \(R\) depends only on membership of \(n,n+1,\dots,n+h\), so
\(\overline{\rm dens}\big(R(S)\triangle R(S_{y,K})\big)\le(h+1)\,\overline{\rm dens}(S\triangle S_{y,K})\).
\(R(S_{y,K})\) is periodic, hence has a density; so \(R(S)\)'s upper and lower densities differ by
\(\le2(h+1)\varepsilon\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\). ∎

So the technicality is **real for general \(S\) and harmless for every \(S\) in play** — and the same
truncation is what makes [E4] Step 4 work.
