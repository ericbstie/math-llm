**Odd `p`.** `p | n` and `p | n+2` are mutually exclusive. `density{v_p(n)=a} = p^{-a}(1-1/p)`, so
```
A_p = (1-2/p) + (1-1/p)[ (1-1/p)^{-z} + (1-1/p)^{-w} - 2 ].
```

**`p = 2` — derived independently, with the `n ≡ 2 (mod 4)` branch done by hand.**

### C.3 Was parity ever expected to be in the main term? **No — the statement is Selberg's principle restated.** (question 3(b))

The main term `M(x;z,w) = S(z,w)·M_z(x)M_w(x)/x` is, by construction, a function of the family of
**local densities** `(A_p)_p` and nothing else. The parity phenomenon (Selberg) is exactly the
assertion that **local densities do not determine the prime count**: there exist weights matching all
sieve/local data up to the level of distribution that are supported on integers with an odd number of
prime factors, hence carry no primes. Therefore:

> *"the parity obstruction is not in the main term"* ≡ *"parity is not in the local densities"* ≡
> **the definition of the parity obstruction.**

There is no localization performed here: the main term was never a candidate location for parity, and
`L-SS-ORIGIN` demonstrating that it is not there is a tautology dressed as a discovery. Nothing in
the (z,w) family is sign-sensitive either: on squarefree `n`, `d_z(n) = z^{ω(n)}`, so the family is
a generating function in `Ω`-type data; the parity twist `λ(n) = (-1)^{Ω(n)}` corresponds to the
reflection `(z,w) ↦ (-z,-w)`, and **nothing in the construction relates the two branches**
(numerically `S(0.3,0.3) = 1.164` vs `S(-0.3,-0.3) = 1.276`; no functional equation).

### C.4 "The main term" is not a well-defined object near the origin (question 3(c))

## Part 6 — ATTACK F (steelman): the strongest downstream statement, and what it costs

I tried to build the best possible case *for* `L-SS-ORIGIN`. Here it is, with its price.

**F.1 The one genuine, provable implication.** For each fixed `x`, `T(x;z,w) = sum_{n<=x} d_z(n)d_w(n+2)`
is a *polynomial* in `(z,w)` (finite sum of polynomials), hence entire. So Cauchy applies:
```
∂_z∂_w T(x;z,w)|_(0,0) = (2πi)^{-2} ∮_{|z|=r} ∮_{|w|=r} T(x;z,w) z^{-2} w^{-2} dz dw.
```
Combining with B.1 gives a real theorem-shaped statement:

> **[SS-CAUCHY]** If for some fixed `r > 0`,
> `sup_{|z|=|w|=r} |T(x;z,w) - M(x;z,w)| = o( r² x / (log x)² )`,
> then `π₂(x) ~ 2Π₂ x/(log x)²` — the Hardy–Littlewood asymptotic.

This is one-directional, provable, and **not** a bound.

**F.2 Its price — it is STRICTLY STRONGER than the target.** At fixed `r`, the hypothesis demands
asymptotics for `sum d_z(n)d_w(n+2)` at **non-integer complex** `z, w` on a whole polycircle. Those
are open, and are **not** implied by HL. As `r → 0` the hypothesis degenerates smoothly into TPC
itself (the main term on the polycircle shrinks like `r²`). Either way there is **no gain**: this is
the same species as the project's existing `[TRACE-SUFF]` / `L-QUB-reform` — a sufficient condition
at least as hard as the goal.

**F.3 Is there a predicted natural boundary in `(z,w)`? NO — and I can rule out the obvious source.**
`log sigma_p = -(z-1)(w-1)/p² + O(1/p³)` **uniformly on compacts of ℂ²** (the `1/p` term cancels
identically — verified symbolically, `c_1 = 0`). Hence `sum_p |log sigma_p|` converges locally
uniformly and the Euler product defines an **entire** function on ℂ². So:

> **No natural boundary in `(z,w)` can come from the singular series.** Any obstruction has to sit in
> the error term.

(`S` does vanish at isolated points — e.g. on the diagonal `z=w=t` the single factor `sigma_3` is
`(4/3)(3/2)^t - 1`, zero at `t = log(3/4)/log(3/2) = -0.7095112913514547`, and `S(-1,-1) = -2.302 < 0`
— but these are individual local factors turning negative, not a boundary.)

**F.4 A route to a bound? Plainly: none.** Nothing in the machinery produces a lower bound for
`π₂(x)`. `S(0,0) > 0` is a statement about a coefficient in a heuristic; the entire difficulty is
in an error term for which no estimate of any kind is known near the origin. **I found no route from
"the main term is exactly right" to any bound, and no sharper statement of where the continuation
fails than "everywhere off the two known lines".**

**F.5 The one honest structural yield.** `S(z,1) ≡ 1` and `S(0,0) = 2Π₂` with `S` entire: the
singular series interpolates analytically and without obstruction from the trivial point to the
Hardy–Littlewood constant. That is a small, true, checkable fact — and it is the *evidence for* the
rename verdict, since it says the (z,w)-direction contains no obstruction at all.
