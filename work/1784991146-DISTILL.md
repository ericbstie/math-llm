## ATTACK 1 — find a move that changes \(h/D\); interrogate the quantifier in [L-DICH](b)

[L-DICH](b) says: *"The dilation moves (U) \((D,h)\mapsto(tD,th)\) and (Dn)
\((D,h)\mapsto(D/g,h/g)\) both preserve \(h/D\); hence \(D\mid h\) is invariant, and for
\(h=2\) the invariant class is \(\{1,2\}\)."* As a statement about those two maps this is a
one-line triviality (\(th/tD=h/D\), \((h/g)/(D/g)=h/D\)). **All of its content is in the
quantifier "every".** That is exactly the shape that has already died twice in this project
([DDS-ENV]; "iff \(d\mid h\)"). I attacked it four ways.

### 1.1 First, the exact closure under which invariance is a theorem (I prove it, sharpened)

> **Claim (mine).** Suppose an identity
> \(\sum_{n\le x,\,n\equiv a(D)}\lambda(n)\lambda(n+h)=\varepsilon\sum_{m\le x',\,m\equiv a'(D')}\lambda(m)\lambda(m+h')\)
> arises from a substitution \(n=tm\) that is a bijection of the **whole** class onto the whole
> image class, with the two \(\lambda\)-factors matched by complete multiplicativity alone.
> Then \(t\mid\gcd(a,D,h)\), \((a',D',h')=(a,D,h)/t\), \(\varepsilon=1\).
>
> *Proof.* Matching by multiplicativity means \(n=um\) and \(n+h=v(m+h')\) with \(u,v\) constant.
> Subtracting, \((u-v)m=vh'-h\) for all \(m\) in an infinite set, so \(u=v=:t\) and \(h=th'\).
> Bijectivity on the whole class forces \(t\mid n\) for **every** \(n\equiv a\ (D)\), i.e.
> \(t\mid D\) and \(t\mid a\). \(\varepsilon=\lambda(t)^2=1\). \(\square\)

So the **complete** invariant of the \(\{(U),(Dn)\}\)-orbit is not \(h/D\) but the **projective
triple** \([a:D:h]\), i.e. the reduced triple \((a,D,h)/\gcd(a,D,h)\); \(h/D\) is a strictly
weaker consequence. This matters immediately: \(\gcd(1,2,2)=1\), so **the odd class \((1,2,2)\)
admits no down-move at all**, even though \(h/D=1\in\mathbb Z\). Under the correct invariant the
orbits are
\[
  (0,1,2)\to(0,1,2)\ [\text{MRT/MR at }d=1],\quad (0,2,2)\to(0,1,1)\ [\text{MR}],\quad
  (0,4,2)\to(0,2,1),\quad (2,4,2)\to(1,2,1),\quad (1,2,2)\to(1,2,2)\ \text{[nothing]}.
\]
**\(h/D\in\mathbb Z\) is necessary but not sufficient for the dilation route.** EXPLORE's
\(\{1,2\}\) conclusion is therefore not even correct *relative to its own move set* at the level
of \((a,D)\) pairs: the dilation route reaches \((0,1)\) and \((0\bmod2,2)\), and **not**
\((1\bmod2,2)\).

### 1.2 Three exact moves outside \(\{(U),(Dn)\}\) that change \(h/D\) (all verified)

* **Splitting.** \(\sum_{n\equiv a(D)}=\sum_{j<t}\sum_{n\equiv a+jD\,(tD)}\) is an exact
  identity requiring **no** multiplicativity, and it multiplies \(h/D\) by \(1/t\).
  EXPLORE's own headline Prop D2 lives on the far side of exactly such a split:
  \((0,2,2)\ [h/D=1]\ \to\ (0,4,2)+(2,4,2)\ [h/D=\tfrac12]\).
* **Restrict-then-divide.** \((1,3,2)\ [h/D=\tfrac23]\to(4,6,2)\to(2,3,1)\ [h/D=\tfrac13]\).
* **A non-dilation \(\lambda\)-convolution identity.** From \(\sum_{d\mid n}\lambda(d)=1_{n=\square}\):
  \[
    \sum_{d\le x}\lambda(d)\!\!\sum_{N\le x,\ d\mid N}\!\!\lambda(N)\lambda(N+2)\;=\;\sum_{k\le\sqrt x}\lambda(k^2+2)
    \;=\;O(\sqrt x).
  \]
  This is a single exact relation **coupling every modulus at once**; it is not in the move
  set, it does not preserve \(h/D\), and it delivers no bound at any individual \(d\).

None of these three transports a bound, so on its own each is only evidence that the closure is
not "all exact identities". The next one *does* transport a bound.

### 1.3 THE BREAK: a bound-producing move outside the move set, reaching \((1,2,2)\)

Take the two **multiplicative** functions
\[
  f_1(n)=\lambda(n)\cdot 1_{n\ \mathrm{odd}},\qquad f_2(n)=\lambda(n)\chi_{-4}(n).
\]
Both are completely multiplicative (products of completely multiplicative functions; \(f_1\) is
\(\lambda\) times the principal character mod 2). Both vanish exactly on the even numbers, so
each is non-zero on a set of density \(1/2\). \(f_1(3)=-1<0\); \(f_2(5)=\lambda(5)\chi_{-4}(5)=-1<0\).
**MR Corollary 3 therefore applies to both**, giving \(\gg x\) sign changes for each.

The non-zero points of both functions are precisely the odd integers, whose consecutive gaps are
**exactly 2**. So a sign change of \(f_i\) is exactly an adjacent pair \((n,n+2)\), \(n\) odd, and
\[
  \#\mathrm{signchanges}(f_1,[1,x])=B:=\#\{n\ \mathrm{odd}\le x-2:\lambda(n)\lambda(n+2)=-1\},
\]
\[
  \#\mathrm{signchanges}(f_2,[1,x])=A:=\#\{n\ \mathrm{odd}\le x-2:\lambda(n)\lambda(n+2)=+1\},
\]
the second because \(\chi_{-4}(n)\chi_{-4}(n+2)=-1\) for **every** odd \(n\) (\(n\) and \(n+2\)
lie in different classes mod 4) — verified as an assertion in code at every \(x\) tested.
Hence \(A\ge\delta_Ax\) and \(B\ge\delta_Bx\) with \(A+B=\tfrac x2+O(1)\), so with
\(\delta:=\min(\delta_A,\delta_B)>0\)
\[
  \boxed{\ \Bigl|\sum_{n\le x,\ n\ \mathrm{odd}}\lambda(n)\lambda(n+2)\Bigr|\ \le\ \Bigl(\tfrac12-2\delta\Bigr)x+O(1).\ }
\]
The trivial bound is \(x/2\). **This is an unconditional constant-factor bound on the odd class
mod 2 at shift 2 — the exact \((a,d)\) pair the sheet records as "still gets nothing".**

**Honesty note.** This is a two-line consequence of a published corollary; I make **no novelty
claim** and I did not search the literature for it. It is offered as an *auditable derivation*,
and the one thing a human should check first is whether it is already in print.

**Why this breaks [L-DICH](b)'s use.** \((1,2,2)\) is in no dilation orbit of a known theorem
(§1.1). The move used — replace \(\lambda\) by a multiplicative function whose **support** or
**real-character twist** cuts out the class — is outside \(\{(U),(Dn)\}\), and it *changes the
reachable set*. So the answer to EXPLORE's own stress-test question is: **no, the move set is
not exhaustive, and here is the object.**

### 1.5 How far does the new move go? (I pushed it; it stops at \(D=2\), rigorously)

Two independent obstructions, both checkable:

* **Support obstruction.** For multiplicative \(f\), \(\{n:f(n)\neq0\}=\{n:(n,Q)=1\}\) for a set
  of primes \(Q\). MR Cor 3 yields a **fixed-shift** correlation statement only when the gaps
  between consecutive elements are constant. Scan: constant **only** for \(Q=\emptyset\) (gap 1)
  and \(Q=\{2\}\) (gap 2). \(Q=\{3\}\to\{1,2\}\), \(\{2,3\}\to\{2,4\}\), \(\{2,3,5\}\to\{2,4,6\}\).
  So this mechanism produces exactly two fixed shifts: 1 (all \(n\)) and 2 (odd \(n\)).
* **Real-character dimension obstruction.** MR Cor 3 needs \(f\) **real**-valued, so the only
  admissible twists are \(\lambda\psi\) with \(\psi\) a **real** character. Isolating one class
  mod \(q\) needs \(\varphi(q)\) independent functionals; the real characters mod \(q\) number
  \(2^{\omega(q)+O(1)}\). Computed: \(q=8\): 4 real characters but the induced sign vectors

I also tested the \(Q=\{p\}\) route directly (\(p=3,5,7\)): the sign-change count splits as a

class is extracted** (numbers below). This is a *rigorous* wall for this mechanism, not an
absence-of-construction: far better than what [C5C-REACH2] had.

## ATTACK 2 — build the non-pretentious counterexample to [TARGET-ROB]

**Setup.** \(f\) completely multiplicative \(\pm1\) \(\iff\) \(a\in\mathbb F_2^{\{\text{primes}\}}\)
with \(f(n)=(-1)^{\sum_p a_pv_p(n)}\). The relation \(f(2m)f(2m+1)=\sigma\) is the **linear**

**2.1 The naive robustification is FALSE, and here is the family.** \(f(n)=(-1)^{v_{p_0}(n)}\)

(This is the analogue of the previous DISTILL's de Bruijn object: it pins the hypothesis.)

**2.2 For \(f\) differing from 1 on a FINITE set \(S\) of primes, [TARGET-ROB] is TRUE and
sharp — I prove it.** For odd \(p\), \(2m\) and \(2m+1\) are coprime, so
\(\mathbb P(v_p(2m(2m+1))\ \text{odd})=\tfrac1{p+1}+\tfrac1{p+1}=\tfrac2{p+1}\), and these events
are independent across distinct \(p\) by CRT. Hence
\[
  \varepsilon(S)=\tfrac12\Bigl(1-\prod_{p\in S}\bigl(1-\tfrac4{p+1}\bigr)\Bigr),
  \qquad\text{so } \varepsilon\to0\iff \sum_{p\in S}\tfrac1p\to0\iff \mathbb D(f,1)^2\to0 .

Verified to 6 decimals at six sets \(S\); the ratio \(\mathbb D(f,1)^2/\varepsilon\) stays in
\([1.01,1.65]\). **So on the finite-\(S\) family the sharp truth is \(\mathbb D(f,1)^2\asymp\varepsilon\).**
Note \(3\in S\) alone already forces \(\varepsilon=1/2\) exactly.

**2.3 The constrained search found no counterexample.** Local search over 17 984 prime variables

## THE CRUX, RESTATED (checkpoint, not exit)

C5 is untouched: \(\sum_{n\le x,\ n\equiv a(d)}\lambda(n)\lambda(n+2)=o(x/d)\) **uniformly**
over \(d\le x^{1-\varepsilon}\) and all \(a\). Everything above lives at \(d\in\{1,2\}\) and at
constant-factor strength — two infinite gaps away.

**What this pass changes about the wall's location.** The wall is no longer "the shift graph on
a class mod \(D\ge3\) is a matching, and \(h/D\) is dilation-invariant" — that framing was
falsified by the odd class, which no dilation reaches and which is nevertheless bounded. The
sharper, primary-source wall is:

> The only proven parity-breaking mechanism converts a correlation hypothesis into a statement
> about **sign changes between consecutive non-zero values of a real multiplicative function**.
> The support of a multiplicative function is \(\{n:(n,Q)=1\}\), whose consecutive gaps are
> constant only for \(Q=\emptyset\) (shift 1) and \(Q=\{2\}\) (shift 2 on the odds); and the
> only class-separating twists that keep \(f\) **real** are the \(2^{\omega(q)+O(1)}\) real
> characters mod \(q\), against the \(\varphi(q)\) functionals needed to isolate one class.

**What would break or escalate it.** Two concrete, well-posed asks, neither a relocation:
1. **A sign-change / equidistribution theorem for multiplicative functions taking values in a
   larger set than \(\{\pm1,0\}\)** — the real-valuedness of \(f\) is what caps the twists at
   \(O(q^{o(1)})\); a complex analogue (an "argument-change" statement for \(\lambda\chi\) with
   \(\chi\) complex) would immediately supply the missing \(\varphi(q)\) functionals. This is
   the precise cohomological-shaped input the wall is asking for.
2. **A multiplicative function whose support has constant gap \(g\ge3\)** — impossible for
   \(\{n:(n,Q)=1\}\) (W1), so this half of the obstruction is closed and the pressure moves
   entirely onto (1).
