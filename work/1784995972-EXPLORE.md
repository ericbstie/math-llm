### 2.1 The candidate lemma

> **[L-REACH-BDRY] (candidate — NOT a legal foundation until DISTILL has attacked it).**
> Let \(S\subseteq\mathbb N\) have natural density \(\sigma>0\), let \(h\ge1\), and set
> \[ R \;:=\; \{n\in S:\ n+h\in S\ \text{and}\ (n,n+h)\cap S=\varnothing\} \]
> (the left endpoints of the **consecutive** pairs of \(S\) at gap exactly \(h\)), with density
> \(\rho\), and \(\gamma:=\sigma-\rho\) (the density of consecutive pairs at gap \(\ne h\)).
> Write \(\partial:=\overline{\mathrm{dens}}\,\big((R+h)\setminus R\big)\).
>
> **(a) (the inequality)** \(R\subseteq S\) and \(R+h\subseteq S\), hence
> \(\sigma\ \ge\ \rho+\partial\), i.e. \(\ \gamma\ \ge\ \partial\).
>
> **(b) (what the mechanism needs)** Let \(f\) be real multiplicative with \(\operatorname{supp}f=S\)
> and let \(\delta=\delta(f)\) be MR Corollary 3's sign-change proportion. Sign changes of \(f\) occur
> only at consecutive pairs of \(S\), and at most \(\gamma x+o(x)\) of them at gaps \(\ne h\); so the
> **direct counting deduction** yields a positive-proportion lower bound on
> \(\#\{n\le x:\ n\in R,\ f(n)f(n+h)=\varepsilon\}\) **only if** \(\delta>\gamma=\sigma-\rho\ \ge\ \partial\).
>
> **(c) (the δ-oblivious case)** Always \(\delta\le\sigma\). The deduction is valid for *arbitrarily
> small* δ **iff** \(\gamma=0\), i.e. \(\rho=\sigma\), i.e. \(R=S\) up to density zero, i.e. **\(R+h=R\)**
> — every consecutive gap of \(S\) equals \(h\).
>
> **(d) (the wall, in full generality)** If \(R\) coincides up to density zero with a **single residue
> class** \(a\bmod d\) and \(d\nmid h\), then \(a+h\not\equiv a\ (d)\), so \(R\) and \(R+h\) are
> **disjoint** classes and \(\partial=\rho=1/d\). By (a), \(\sigma\ge 2/d=2\rho\), hence
> \[ \boxed{\ \rho\ \le\ \sigma/2\quad\text{and}\quad \gamma\ \ge\ \sigma/2\ } \]
> with **equality iff \(S=(a\bmod d)\cup(a+h\bmod d)\) up to density zero.** Consequently the direct
> deduction requires \(\delta>\sigma/2\) — **strictly more sign changes than the independence
> (Chowla) model predicts** — for *every* set \(S\), *every* shift \(h\), and *every* modulus \(d\nmid h\).
>
> **(e) (the exact room bound for unions of classes)** If \(R\) is a union of \(k\) classes mod \(d\)
> and \(m:=|R\cup(R+h)|\) as a subset of \(\mathbb Z/d\), then \(\sigma\ge m/d\), \(\rho=k/d\), and
> since \(\sigma\mapsto\sigma/(2(\sigma-k/d))\) is decreasing,
> \[ \text{room}\ :=\ \frac{\sigma/2}{\gamma}\ \le\ \frac{m}{2(m-k)}\ =\ \frac{1+\partial/\rho}{2\,\partial/\rho}. \]
> **In particular room \(>1\) (the Chowla value suffices) iff \(\partial<\rho\)**: the reached set must
> be *more than half invariant* under \(+h\). A single class with \(d\nmid h\) sits exactly at
> \(\partial=\rho\) — the worst possible case.

**Proof.** (a) By definition \(n\in R\Rightarrow n\in S\) and \(n+h\in S\); \(R\) and \((R+h)\setminus R\)
are disjoint subsets of \(S\). (b) Each \(n\in S\) is the left endpoint of exactly one consecutive
pair, so consecutive pairs have density \(\sigma\) and those at gap \(\ne h\) density \(\gamma\); each
carries at most one sign change; MR gives \(\ge\delta x\) in total. (c) \(\delta\le\sigma\) because the
number of sign changes is at most the number of consecutive pairs; "valid for every \(\delta>0\)"
forces \(\gamma=0\); \(\gamma=0\iff\rho=\sigma\iff\) every element of \(S\) has its successor at
distance \(h\) \(\iff R=S\) and \(R+h=R\). (d) as displayed. (e) \(R\cup(R+h)\subseteq S\) gives
\(\sigma\ge m/d\); monotonicity is \(\frac{d}{d\sigma}\frac{\sigma}{2(\sigma-c)}=\frac{-2c}{4(\sigma-c)^2}<0\)
for \(c=k/d>0\). ∎
